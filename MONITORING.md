# Monitoring definitions

Fixed exploratory pilot: Israel (ISR), Palestinian territories (PSE), Lebanon, Ukraine, Sudan, Ethiopia, Somalia and Yemen. This deliberately chosen pilot is not a representative world sample. Source territory labels remain authoritative: a shared country code does not make different footprints interchangeable.

Configuration is versioned in `monitoring/config.json`. Normalized observations retain country, observation start/end, metric, value, unit, numerator/denominator, frequency, source ID/version/URL, available publication date, retrieval date, provisional status, source dimensions and quality note. Missing publication dates remain missing. Manifests retain request hashes and observation/projection bounds. IPC now reads the full national assessment history, preserves prior source versions and quarantines identities that cannot be resolved from the export.

## Derived measures and testing

**Food affordability.** Compare retail staple mass purchasable with one same-market, same-month, same-currency daily non-qualified wage. Commodity, market, price type, currency and units are preserved. This is a local purchasing-power measure, not a numerical fulfillment threshold for Revelation 6:6.

**Food-price changes.** Compare each retail staple series with itself exactly twelve calendar months earlier. Fix series membership using at least 24 observations in 2015–2019. Monthly aggregation requires at least 70% of fixed series and at least three series; annual diagnostics require nine eligible months. The median matched-series change is not CPI or a nationally representative basket.

**Regional lag family.** Conflict deaths per 100,000 → net refugee-stock change; conflict → IPC 3+; retail staple price change → IPC 3+. Eight countries × three pairs × lags 0/1/2 years = 72 fixed tests. Positive lag means predictor earlier than response. Longest contiguous finite overlap must have at least twelve years. Signed-log measures are separately detrended; three-year block permutations provide exploratory p-values; BH keeps all 72 cells in the family. Missing IPC history or uncertain geography leaves those tests unavailable.

**Regional synchrony.** Four fixed domains: conflict, food prices, influenza sentinel positivity, religious restrictions. Baseline 2000–2019, at least ten observed baseline years; extreme threshold z > 1.5, compound screen at least two domains. Every fixed domain must be eligible. Dependence-aware calibration requires sufficient joint observations; coverage failure remains unavailable. The annual panel remains unchanged. A separate weekly seasonal influenza monitor is described below; household exposure to several hazards remains unmeasured.

**Israel follow-up.** Area and yield separate agricultural expansion from per-hectare response. The 54 historical crop/rain tests and 30 follow-up decomposition tests remain separate declared families. Irrigation sensitivity uses actual irregular source observations and calendar-distance HAC. Rain × era tests interaction directly. The future holdout remains frozen; no later year can validate until corresponding rain and crop observations both exist.

**Current Israel climate.** CRU-CY4.10 supplies five monthly variables under the publisher's national area weighting. October–September rainfall and rain minus day-adjusted PET use a fixed 1991–2020 baseline. March–May average daily maximum temperature is a seasonal heat proxy, not a count of extreme-heat days. Same-version CRU-CY versus CCKP overlap differs materially despite high correlation; the products are not interchangeable. A separate exploratory five-coefficient heat/irrigation family leaves the original model unchanged. Future validation still requires at least ten compatible annual pairs from 2025 onward.

**Displacement flows.** IDMC's annual movements and year-end stocks are displayed as separate measures. Neither is summed with UNHCR stocks or with separate IDMC disaster-event rows. The existing 72-test family is unchanged; cross-source inference needs separately verified territorial and cause definitions.

**IPC history and revisions.** The selected resource is `ipc_global_national_long.csv`, described by HDX as all national data, rather than `ipc_global_national_long_latest.csv`. [IPC's population tracking documentation](https://www.ipcinfo.org/ipc-country-analysis/population-tracking-tool/en/) describes history since 2017; actual supplied coverage varies by country. In this pilot the earliest supplied periods start in 2021; Israel and Ukraine have no IPC observations. Lebanon and Yemen start in 2022, and PSE in 2023. No prehistory is synthesized and no twelve-year eligibility requirement is shortened.

The export provides country, analysis-month label, validity type, dates, phase counts and assessed-population totals. It does **not** provide publisher analysis IDs or assessed-boundary geometry. Local period keys are explicitly derived from country + analysis label + validity type + start/end dates; `source_analysis_id` remains null. Only unambiguous matching periods replace prior records. Revisions replace the whole matching period, so a phase explicitly changed to missing does not inherit its previous value. Other periods remain separate, including a projection sharing dates with a current assessment. Export modification time is labeled as such; it is not the assessment publication date.

The September 2026 full-history export has conflicting rows for Ethiopia's May 2021 current/projection periods and Somalia's January 2026 current/two projection periods. Ethiopia's current denominators differ by about 47 million people; Somalia's differ slightly but the export provides no authoritative rule for choosing one. These five ambiguous periods are quarantined. Eight Ethiopia observations from the prior latest-only export independently pass count-partition checks and remain explicitly labeled prior snapshots, with their original URL, denominator and retrieval time; they are not presented as an unambiguous new history match.

One further period has a confirmed denominator contradiction: Sudan's January 2026 analysis, first projection covering June–September 2026. Its five phase counts sum to 8,289,610, while phase-all reports 47,535,794. Both the full-history and original latest-only exports contain this conflict. All four corresponding active observations are excluded, including previously ingested rows; the denominator is not replaced with the phase sum. Six periods (77 original rows) now sit in `data/diagnostics/ipc_history/`, outside the observation loader. The original 48-row and interim 388-row observation snapshots and manifests are preserved by content hash, including their now-excluded values.

When all phase counts exist, phases 1–5 must agree with phase-all, and phases 3–5 with phase 3+. Partial phase sums must not exceed their reported total. The conservative consistency tolerance is the greater of five people or 1.5% of the assessed population, matching the existing 1.5 percentage-point ratio screen and allowing publisher rounding/process differences. This is a data-quality screen, not an assertion of count precision. Replaying unchanged data creates no additional revision archive. A source-row shrink beyond 10% fails before changing the active snapshot.

HDX also supplies full level-1 and area histories. Those tables contain names but no stable geographic identifiers or geometry; overlapping population groups and changing assessed coverage prevent an automatic boundary match. They are not aggregated into national totals here. Every IPC observation keeps `geography_comparable_to_country=false`; the expanded history improves descriptive assessment records, without unlocking the existing regional lag tests.

## Climate, seasonal influenza and purchasing-power extensions

The [comparison plan](monitoring/extension_plan.json) was committed before inspecting these extension results. Historical data already existed, so this is an exploratory design freeze, not preregistration before observation. Existing global/regional families, coverage gates and the frozen Israel wheat model remain separate.

**Climate sources.** NOAA CPC [monthly relative Niño3.4](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/data/indices/Rnino34.ascii.txt) and [seasonal RONI](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/data/indices/RONI.ascii.txt) use Relative ERSSTv6 and the 1991–2020 base period. NOAA PSL [DMI](https://psl.noaa.gov/data/timeseries/month/data/dmi.had.long.data) uses HadISST1.1. Monthly RNI supplies the ENSO control; RONI is displayed over its full three-month observation window. Annual RNI/DMI means require all twelve January–December months in a completed year. Raw inputs and definitions are archived by hash; four validated tables activate together. Changed product/baseline, inconsistent RNI/RONI windows, lost observations or damaged archives fail before replacing the active snapshot. Historical exports need a separate output root if they would reduce active coverage. Current revised vintages do not reconstruct historical release-time predictors.

**Climate sensitivity.** Reuse all 45 global pairs with both a shared time/regime baseline and a climate-adjusted fit: 90 planned tests. Separately, reuse all 72 regional country/pair/lag cells with both fits: 144 tests. Each pair of fits uses identical longest contiguous finite samples, including climate coverage. Minimum sample sizes remain 20 global and 12 regional years, with at least eight residual degrees of freedom. Lagged regional fits include climate in both predictor and response years. HAC3 slope tests use finite-sample correction; BH includes unavailable cells as p=1. Partial correlations, pointwise intervals, exact years and reasons for ineligibility are retained. Shared nuisance terms differ from the original matrix's separately detrended block-null procedure, so this is a sensitivity, not replacement inference.

The first run has 72 eligible global fits and 34 eligible regional fits. No global fit passes BH. Ukraine's same-year conflict-death rate / refugee-stock-change relationship passes both HAC fits (2001–2025; baseline r=0.593, climate-adjusted r=0.590; q=0.0048). The original block-permutation family gives q=0.36 for that cell. Evidence therefore depends on inference method. War/famine is already weak in the new 1950–2023 baseline (r=0.053; adjusted r=0.057; both q=1), before climate terms are added. Its difference from the original 1900–2023 finding cannot be attributed to ENSO/IOD adjustment.

**Seasonal influenza.** Keep WHO SENTINEL observations separate by complete country/source/version/dimension identity. Require integer positive/tested counts, a full Monday–Sunday week, a seven-day reporting lag and at least 100 specimens. For each of the latest 52 expected weeks, compare with the corresponding seasonal window (±2 weeks) in the prior five ISO years. Each reference window needs four eligible weeks; at least three windows must qualify. Pool specimens within each window and take the median positivity across qualified windows. Every reference week precedes the target and belongs to an earlier ISO year; week 53 maps to week 52 only when that historical year lacks week 53. Report positivity, percentage-point difference and ratio only when defined. Missing countries/weeks, partial reports and low-testing weeks remain explicit. WHO's universal provisional flag indicates revision risk; reporting-site coverage is still unverified. No diagnostic or calibrated outbreak alert is inferred.

**Food purchasing power.** Matched actual WFP retail staple and non-qualified daily wage quotes retain original food units and a canonical wage identity. Distinct wage IDs/names/price types cannot be pooled; only duplicates within the exact quote series use a median. DAY and 1 DAY labels are equivalent, with original labels preserved. Exact series yield kg per daily wage and MoM/YoY changes using exact calendar comparators. The 2015–2019 baseline requires at least 36 months and six months in every year. Fixed monitored-market indices give equal weight to markets and fixed staples within each market, and require 100% member coverage; annual results need all twelve months. Competing baseline identities for the same market/staple prevent aggregate membership. No changing basket, currency splice or filled gaps.

The initial snapshot provides 7,474 matched observations across 79 exact series, with 7,160 MoM and 6,357 YoY comparisons. No country has an eligible aggregate basket: Yemen has no 2015 pairs, and Ethiopia only has August 2015 pairs. Those baseline rules remain intact. Individual market trends are descriptive and nonrepresentative; they are not national purchasing power or a fulfillment threshold.

[Extension report](results/monitoring/extensions/extensions_report.md) links all numerical results, baseline windows, exclusions, source snapshots and manifests. Routine refreshes use an exact timestamp with an America/Chicago report date; explicit historical date exports include the entire Chicago date and still disclose revised-source limitations.

## Commands and cadence

```bash
python refresh_monitoring.py                    # all configured public adapters
python refresh_monitoring.py --sources ipc who  # selected sources
python refresh_monitoring.py --offline          # replay saved source responses
python sync_feeders.py                          # verified sibling snapshots, including Israel
python sync_feeders.py --check                  # detect drift; no writes
python refresh_climate_indices.py               # versioned NOAA climate snapshot
python refresh_climate_indices.py --offline     # replay cached climate responses
python monitor_extensions.py                    # separate extension report
python monitor_regional.py                      # panel, lag family, synchrony and all reports
```

Daily polling protects the short SWPC observation window; annual feeds will often be unchanged. SWPC's seven-day retention requires successful polls no more than seven days apart to avoid gaps. Annual inferential analyses still exclude partial years. Scheduling is separate from Make and the runner: use `make publish` for an authorized scheduled publication, with a clean checkout and explicit runtime. Notify on failed stages, meaningful revisions, new usable observation periods or changed analytical eligibility; retrieval timestamps and unchanged figure renders do not warrant alerts. See [operations](OPERATIONS.md).

Outputs: `results/monitoring/monitoring_report.md`, `regional_annual_panel.csv`, `regional_lag_tests.csv` and `regional_synchrony.csv`, plus `results/monitoring/extensions/`; source observations remain available for narrower review.

## Shipping, currencies and cereal-import dependence

The [trade report](results/monitoring/trade/trade_report.md) uses three additional versioned source streams. IMF PortWatch supplies daily all-vessel estimated transit at Suez Canal, Bab el-Mandeb and Strait of Hormuz. Source IDs, all pages, source counts and edit markers are verified before activation. The full expected date/route grid retains missing days. Complete calendar-month means require every day, with a project-defined seven-day elapsed reporting lag. These AIS-derived payload estimates can revise; they measure neither grain cargo nor national imports.

World Bank GEM source 15, `DPANUSLCU`, supplies explicitly monthly official LCU/USD period averages. Country IDs and indicator metadata must agree. Annual records, gap filling, nonpositive rates, incompatible currencies and lost observed history block activation or comparison. Official rates may differ from market rates. FAOSTAT food-security item 21035, element 6121, supplies three-year cereal-import dependence. Full windows, source flags and negative net-exporter ratios remain explicit; the ratio is descriptive context and never interpolated into a monthly predictor.

The [frozen trade plan](monitoring/trade_plan.json) specifies eight countries, four predictors, lags 0/1/3 months and two fits: 192 tests, including unavailable cells as p=1 in BH. Shipping predictor is fractional YoY decline; FX predictor and food response are natural-log YoY changes. Both fits share the longest contiguous complete overlap including all adjustment controls, with earliest tie-breaking, at least 60 months and eight residual degrees of freedom. Baseline includes trend and calendar-month effects. Adjustment adds monthly RNI/DMI and applicable official FX in predictor/response months. HAC uses 12 Bartlett lags, finite-sample correction and Student-t inference. Native-unit slopes, pointwise intervals, partial correlations, exact samples and exclusions are exported.

Food membership and observed-month coverage preserve the original 2015–2019 fixed price-basket rules. Initial live parity checks reproduce all 27 original annual basket results within the new monitor's 2015-onward scope. The September 6 snapshot has six estimable Yemen FX fits and no corrected survivor; shipping overlap is insufficient. Related routes, overlapping YoY windows and common crisis shocks prevent causal interpretation.

[Source recovery evidence](SOURCE_RECOVERY.md) preserves the separate WFP/IPC archive audit. It does not add imputed wages, infer historical identifiers from names, or relax the original affordability and geographic gates.

## Complete eclipse catalog and Jerusalem visibility

The [celestial monitor](data/celestial_monitoring/data/eclipses/report.md) preserves all 454 solar and 459 lunar NASA catalog events from 1900 through 2100, including future predictions and all published eclipse types. The selected 63-event historical input remains separate. One fixed NASA Jerusalem point (31°46′ N, 35°14′ E; source elevation 808.9 m) supplies modeled local contacts, altitude, magnitude, obscuration and phase durations. This location is not an all-Israel visibility footprint.

Pinned unmodified NASA JSEX/JLEX programs and century elements run with Node 26.8.1. The frozen feeder plan defines horizon conventions, contact dates across midnight, Gregorian calendar and modeled UT1 (source TD minus ΔT). UT1 output is not exact leap-second-aware UTC. Lunar global maximum can fall below Jerusalem's horizon; the report labels that case. Full phase duration and above-horizon duration are distinct. Source lunar altitude does not use elevation, and modeled penumbral visibility does not guarantee naked-eye detection. Terrain, weather and visual contrast are unmodeled.

One published Jerusalem solar case and three fixed lunar contact/map cases validate the wrapper within predeclared tolerances; they do not constitute independent physics or precise lunar-altitude validation. Global identity/count checks include noncentral eclipse-type modifiers and century boundaries. Failed checks stop promotion. Central synchronization verifies all output hashes and source/code/plan bindings before copying any feeder data. Original statistical families and future wheat-validation requirements remain unchanged.
